A stack decision should explain what the next maintainer is signing up for.

Before comparing backend languages or frontend frameworks, write the requirements that could eliminate a candidate. Then compare two or three configurations you could actually build.

For each one, record:

1. Where the application runs and who owns accepted state.
2. Why its frontend rendering approach fits the pages you need.
3. The maintenance and deployment consequences you accept.
4. What is documented, observed, preferred, or still unknown.

In a fictional request tracker, familiarity with TypeScript might justify a provisional Node.js backend. It does not prove that pending notification work survives a restart. That needs a separate check in the intended configuration.

Give a spike one decision-changing question, an effort cap, and a rule for interpreting the result. Keep incomplete evidence visible.

Save the reason for the choice and the trigger that would reopen it. Another framework announcement does not need to become another migration.
