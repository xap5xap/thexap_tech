If your idea gets no replies, first check whether the intended people ever encountered it.

That changes what you can learn from the silence.

Putting a page online gives you a destination. It does not tell you whether someone with the problem saw it, understood it or wanted to act.

Before building, write a small discovery brief:

Who experiences the problem?
Which route can you actually use to reach them?
What question could change your next decision?
What useful artifact or conversation can you offer?
When will you stop and review?

For a fictional shared request tracker, the first test could be a few conversations with people who coordinate team requests. Ask about a recent handoff before showing the solution. Offer a short handoff checklist afterward if wanted.

Give access its own checkpoint. Start observing responses from confirmed exposure, while keeping a final closeout date and total effort cap.

No exposure tells you about access. A misunderstood explanation calls for a revision. A repeated recent problem can justify a prototype test.

Each result leaves different questions open. A signup still does not establish willingness to pay.

Choose the next step from the evidence you actually collected.
