# Audience access and discovery experiment worksheet

Use this worksheet to plan one small test before committing to a product build. You will leave with a reachable audience, a question, a finite plan and a way to interpret the result. Keep access, attention, comprehension, demand and willingness to pay separate.

## Inputs and instructions

Bring an opportunity brief, or a provisional sentence naming who experiences the problem and when. List the introductions, communities or destinations you could realistically use. Access and permission may still be unknown.

Complete the plan before you contact anyone. Use the example as a guide, not as a set of required numbers. During the test, record what happened in the result log. At closeout, apply the decision guide and write one next action with its evidence limit. The original Markdown and editable document let you expand the writing spaces.

## Completed planning example

**Fictional example, planned only. No people were contacted and no results are claimed.** The product idea is a shared request tracker for small teams.

- **Audience:** someone who currently coordinates incoming team requests and handles handoffs.
- **Assumption and question:** recent handoffs require reconstructing ownership or status. Do coordinators describe a recent occurrence and an effort to resolve it?
- **Primary route:** permissioned introductions to people in this role. In this example, whether introductions are available is still an access assumption to check.
- **Alternative:** a relevant operations community after checking its current research rules and obtaining permission where needed.
- **Useful offer:** a request-handoff checklist containing the request, owner, status and next action. Offer it after asking about the existing process.
- **Request:** a voluntary ten-minute conversation about a recent handoff, without a signup or product promise.
- **Evidence:** confirm fit and exposure, ask about a recent handoff and current workaround, and record the account without private workplace details. Product comprehension is not tested unless an explanation is shown separately.
- **Usable coverage:** five suitable conversations before applying the three-of-five pattern below. Count different people once.
- **Capacity:** four hours total; up to five conversations and ten permissioned invitations, with at most one brief follow-up per invitation where welcome. Reserve time for review. Use existing materials and tools; pause if spending would be needed.
- **Timing:** access checkpoint on day 7 after starting. Observe for up to seven days after first confirmed exposure, ending by day 14 after starting, or earlier when five conversations finish. Stop sooner at the effort cap. Record a shortened window if exposure arrives late.
- **Decision:** if at least three of five suitable conversations describe a recent occurrence plus an existing workaround, prepare a focused prototype test. Otherwise review the record to revise the situation, change the route or defer. Insufficient coverage stays inconclusive.
- **Notes:** keep only a role description, generalized problem notes, material version and response stage. Keep contact details separately with the person's permission, only as long as needed for the agreed conversation; remove them at closeout unless a specific later contact was agreed.

These numbers are illustrative choices for a small learning exercise, not validated benchmarks or statistical evidence. The alternative route requires a revised plan before use. No outcome has been observed.

## Interpret and close the test

- **No confirmed exposure:** review access and permission. This cannot establish lack of demand.
- **Exposure but insufficient usable responses:** review the invitation, timing, trust, relevance and route. Record uncertainty about the cause.
- **Explanation misunderstood:** revise the explanation, give it a new version and keep the responses separate. Record comprehension as not tested when no explanation was shown.
- **Usable coverage but weak recent-problem pattern:** reconsider or defer the tested assumption, preserving the sample's limits.
- **Repeated recent problem meeting the rule:** prepare a prototype test. Product usability, market demand and willingness to pay remain open.

## Completion check and simpler option

Before running the test, check that another person can identify the audience, primary and alternative routes, question, permission needs, evidence, effort cap and stop rule. Confirm that the rule handles both no exposure and insufficient responses.

After running it, reconcile attempts and responses, label unknowns, record actual exposure and effort, and choose one next action. Closeout can be complete with an inconclusive result. Fulfill any agreed contact and cleanup.

If you already have one willing relevant person, start with one recent-event conversation. Keep the question, evidence note and next decision; omit a campaign, signup form or channel matrix. A discovery exercise reduces a specific uncertainty. It does not prove a business or predict conversion.
