# Project charter and resource register

Use this worksheet to name decision owners, record constraints, and plan resource handover or shutdown before a commitment. Completing it creates a reviewable plan. It does not create accounts, approve spending or perform access changes.

## Inputs and instructions

Bring your intended outcome, current stage, people involved, existing or proposed resources, and known time, spending and data limits. An opportunity brief is useful but optional.

Fill the charter first. Write one accountable owner for each material decision, even when the same person fills several roles. Then copy the resource card for every required resource. Use a custody reference instead of a credential. Label unknown values as unknown and planned resources as planned.

Finish with open questions and a walkthrough of one ordinary change. Do not create accounts, buy services, change permissions or delete resources as part of this exercise. Those actions need their own applicable decisions and checks.

## Completed illustrative charter

This example is fictional. Its values are choices for demonstrating the worksheet, not actual costs, account states or outcomes.

- Purpose: Show each accepted request's owner, status and next action in a shared request tracker.
- Stage: Local demonstration using invented requests; no runtime result is claimed.
- Included work: One request flow and a review of its ownership and state.
- Non-goals: Public release, customer records and automatic messages.
- Scope: Product owner decides. Builder refines within the accepted flow.
- Design: Builder chooses labels and spacing. Changes to the flow return to the product owner.
- Spending: Product owner decides before any commitment; this exercise buys nothing.
- Release: Product owner decides after the builder supplies the agreed checks.
- Time: Review a proposed hosted trial one month after its authorized start.
- Cost: Illustrative US$10 monthly hosting estimate, US$0 estimated setup, usage charges unknown. Actual account pricing and settings remain unverified.
- Budget: Illustrative US$30 ceiling for one month. Owner checks total forecast before spending; exceeding it or unresolved charges pauses that commitment.
- Data: Invented requests only. Real account metadata needs separate consideration before hosting.
- Exit: Product owner decides stop or extension; builder executes a separately authorized scoped plan.
- Review: Version 1. Review before shared access, hosting, paid commitment or public release.

## Completed illustrative resource cards

**Repository**

- Purpose and status: Store the tracker source; planned private repository.
- Owner and operator: Product owner controls it; builder maintains the code.
- Access: Builder needs contribution access. Actual account type and permissions are unverified.
- Custody: Owner's private credential store; no credentials in this card.
- Check: Planned owner access and recovery review before relying on the repository.
- Costs: No paid repository plan included; actual settings still need checking.
- Renewal: Owner checks before enabling any paid option.
- Handover: Owner verifies the receiving operator can maintain the code and reviews outgoing access.
- Exit: Owner authorizes the builder's scoped access removal; code retention is an owner decision.
- Verification: After authorized action, owner checks remaining access against the agreed list. Not performed in this example.

**Test hosting and data store**

- Purpose and status: A future isolated trial environment; planned, not created.
- Owner and operator: Product owner controls the account; builder maintains this environment.
- Access and custody: Minimum necessary operator access, credentials in private custody. Provider roles and recovery remain unverified.
- Costs: US$10 monthly illustration; usage charges and renewal settings unknown.
- Review: Owner checks billing before setup and reviews the trial one month after its start.
- Handover: Owner verifies the receiving operator's access and ability to maintain this environment.
- Exit: Owner decides data disposition; builder stops activity and ends the scoped resource when authorized. Preserve unrelated resources.
- Verification: Check environment state, remaining access and billing after authorized shutdown. Provider retention limits may remain. No shutdown was performed.

**External illustration**

- Purpose and status: Optional public artwork; proposed, not licensed for this example.
- Owner and operator: Product owner decides use; builder maintains the source and approved export.
- Access and custody: Builder needs the approved asset only. No account credential belongs in the project record.
- Costs and renewal: Unknown. Product owner checks license terms and any recurring commitment before use.
- Handover: Retain the permitted source and terms for the receiving operator if allowed.
- Exit: Owner directs removal from future public exports if use is refused; existing use would require its own disposition.
- Verification: Check the relevant exports after the authorized change. No public use is claimed here.

## Completion check

Walk through a paid renewal without buying anything. In the example, the product owner decides; the builder supplies the account's actual costs and settings; the one-month US$30 ceiling applies; unknown usage charges must be resolved; the owner checks the resulting approved commitment against the forecast. This is a walkthrough, not a completed renewal.

For your own project, repeat that check with a new collaborator, release or shutdown. Each required resource passes the planning check only when it has an owner, purpose, cost or renewal check, and specific exit action. Every unresolved question needs an owner and a blocked commitment. Access, recovery, billing and shutdown checks remain planned until performed and recorded.

## Simplify and maintain

For a solo local experiment, use six lines: purpose, stage, decision owner, time and spending limits, permitted data, and review or stop trigger. Omit unused resources. Expand before shared access, external services or recurring charges.

Review the record when ownership, audience, data, cost or stage changes. Preserve old versions. This worksheet does not prove account recovery, security, compliance, provider erasure or product readiness.
