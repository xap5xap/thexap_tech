Before you add a collaborator or pay for hosting, make one small project record useful.

It should answer five ordinary questions:

Who decides?
Who does the work?
What limit applies?
What must be checked first?
How do we hand it over or stop?

A compact charter names the owners of scope, design, spending and release. The same person can fill every role, but finishing the work still leaves a separate release decision.

Add a resource register for the repository, environments and services you actually need. Give each one a purpose, an owner, an access check, a cost or renewal check, and an exit action.

Keep credentials in private custody. A register needs a safe reference, not a token.

Unknowns belong in the record too. "Billing not checked" is more useful than assuming a selected plan guarantees the final bill. Give each unresolved question an owner and name the commitment that waits for its answer.

Then walk through one change without doing it: a renewal, a new collaborator or a shutdown. If you still need to guess who acts, revise that row.

The guide includes a fictional request tracker example and a small charter you can adapt.
