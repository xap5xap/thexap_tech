## How this establishes authority

StoryBrand describes the guide through empathy and authority. Apply that by showing the customer's situation and the evidence of competent help. A founder should be able to recognize a relevant problem, inspect the product, follow the decisions, and understand the result and its limits. This application to the portfolio is our strategic interpretation of the framework. [StoryBrand introduction](https://storybrand.com/downloads/your-brand-is-not-the-hero.pdf)

Xavier already has experience and a real client outcome. The missing piece is a broader, accessible body of relevant work samples. Upwork explicitly recommends selecting work relevant to the work sought and permits personal/sample projects as portfolio material. Such work demonstrates capability within its actual scope; it does not establish client adoption. [Upwork portfolio guidance](https://www.upwork.com/resources/career-portfolio-basics)

Keep four kinds of evidence distinct:

| Evidence | What it can establish | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Working product and inspectable artifacts | Implemented behavior and craft | Demand or sustained reliability |
| Decisions, evaluations, and release records | Judgment and verification within stated conditions | Universal safety or superiority |
| Actual use and task observations | Usefulness for those observed users | Broad market demand |
| Relevant client reference and approved outcome | Credibility in that engagement | Repeated success across every product category |

Public documentation makes evidence easier to evaluate. Posting frequency itself does not establish authority. Edelman and LinkedIn's 2025 report supports the broader idea that substantive content can influence B2B vendor consideration. Its global B2B sample is adjacent evidence, not proof that a particular posting channel or cadence will produce contracts for Xavier. [Report overview](https://www.edelman.com/expertise/Business-Marketing/2025-b2b-thought-leadership-report)

### Proofline: the first recommended new product

**Working audience:** a founder or product marketer preparing a factual B2B software article from documentation and research. This audience is a hypothesis within the existing XAP-61 creator/reviewer scope.

**Situation:** a draft sounds convincing, but the reviewer needs to determine whether each important statement is supported by the supplied material. The burden and willingness to adopt another tool still need observation.

**Transformation to test:** turn a draft and source pack into a reviewable set of claims, supporting passages, unresolved questions, and recorded human decisions.

**One complete workflow:**

1. Open a sample article or provide a short draft and a bounded source pack.
2. Extract statements that can be checked against those sources.
3. Inspect each statement beside the exact supporting passage.
4. See proposed review labels such as supported, overstated, conflicting, or not established by these sources.
5. Accept, edit, reject, or leave a statement unresolved.
6. Save the reviewed version and export the review record.

“Not established by these sources” must not be presented as “false.” Conflicting evidence and inaccessible material need visible states. The tool offers assistance; the reviewer retains the publishing decision.

Start with short text and a few supplied sources. Defer web-wide truth checking, complex PDF/OCR support, browser extensions, autonomous publication, billing, collaboration, and a universal research platform. Add a dependency only when needed for this workflow. Public access, providers, storage, data boundaries, and operating costs are choices for the later build brief.

**Why this is a useful AI demonstration:** the difficult product work is the relationship between a claim, its source, uncertainty, version history, and a human decision. A polished split-pane review interface makes that behavior visible. The surrounding application must preserve work, handle failed processing, avoid duplicate effects on retry, and explain what remains unfinished.

**Existing alternative:** Google's source-based research assistant already offers answers grounded in supplied material with citations. Proofline should test a claim-by-claim review and approval workflow, not assume citations or source chat are novel. The ALCE research separately evaluates citation quality, which reinforces the need to test support rather than equate a citation with correctness. Neither source establishes unmet demand for Proofline. [Google's official source-grounding guide](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16164461?hl=en), [ALCE research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627)

**Evidence to earn:**

- A real recorded progression from the first useful prototype to the implemented release, with scope decisions and actual issues found. Never introduce defects to stage a rescue narrative.
- A versioned evaluation set containing supported claims, unsupported extrapolations, conflicting passages, numerical errors, and source text that attempts to redirect the model.
- A human-labeled answer key reviewed before final evaluation; a held-out portion that is not used for prompt tuning. Publish disagreements and remaining ambiguity.
- Counts of unsupported claims incorrectly marked supported, supported claims incorrectly flagged, and cases left unresolved. Report denominators and example failures, rather than one unexplained accuracy percentage.
- A comparison with manual review and a general assistant using the same source pack. Record review time and error types, counterbalance task order where practical, and disclose the small sample. A weak result is still a real learning outcome.
- Recorded recovery from interrupted processing and a saved review that survives refresh. If shared access is later added, verify authorization and isolation as part of that scope.
- Measured latency and cost under named inputs and configuration, plus a release/handoff note describing operating limits. Do not promise enterprise readiness.

An initial feasibility evaluation might use 30 to 50 deliberately varied claims. That is a proposed development sample, not a certification or evidence of population-level accuracy. Agree on acceptable error severity and release conditions before evaluating the final version.

**Distribution:** show a concrete example where a source exists but does not support the sentence attributed to it. Offer a safe seeded demo and the evaluation method. Intended test users include B2B writers and product marketers; intended portfolio readers include founders and product leads considering AI product work.

**Learning signal:** can reviewers finish the workflow and correctly explain its limits; does it catch relevant issues without unacceptable false reassurance; does anyone choose it again for another draft? Separately track whether prospective clients can identify the engineering judgment demonstrated and start a relevant conversation.

**Status:** recommended candidate, not selected or validated. A small concept can earn implementation and evaluation evidence before it earns adoption evidence.

### Operational workflow: audit existing work before building another product

XAP-50 describes a publishing system, but an issue description is not evidence that every listed automation works. Audit actual input/output artifacts, approvals, state tracking, failures, and repeated use. If it already has a coherent operated workflow, it may supply the third flagship faster than another build.

If it is mostly a collection of prompts and manual steps, describe that honestly. Do not build a generalized content platform merely to make the case study sound more impressive. If Proofline becomes a component of this system, explain the relationship and avoid counting overlapping evidence as two independent successes.

If a separate operational concept is still needed, consider a **client onboarding and approval workspace** for a small service business: request a defined set of inputs, let the client submit them, resolve missing information, approve a specific version, and produce a handoff record. The founder of the hypothetical software product and its service-business users are distinct from Xavier's consulting audience.

This can demonstrate roles, persistent workflow state, revisions, reminders, approval history, and recovery. Start with one operator/client journey, sample files, and controlled notifications. Avoid full CRM, payments, signature products, and broad integrations. Human-confirmed completion is a useful product decision; AI is optional and must earn its place.

HoneyBook already provides client portals for shared files, messages, and project details. A proposed narrower approval workflow needs a concrete underserved scenario before becoming a business bet. For portfolio purposes, the evidence would be the implemented decisions and tested behavior. [HoneyBook client portal documentation](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/6428603-what-clients-can-see-and-do-in-the-client-portal)

A possible distribution experiment is a short approval-conflict demo aimed at small-team operators, followed by observed completion of the same task. Useful learning is whether participants understand the next action and use the approved version correctly. Demand, paid adoption, and reduced project delays would require separate evidence.

This is a new brainstorm example outside the current XAP-54 candidate set. It needs an explicit candidate-list decision before formal selection. It is not a parallel build recommendation.

## Improve the portfolio page

1. **Give the current flagship appropriate space.** Use a large feature with application and brand visuals, a concrete transformation, current status, ownership summary, and one approved proof point. Do this even while only one case exists.
2. **Make each eventual card answer a different buyer question.** Armonía: can he turn a real operating problem into adopted software? Proofline: can he make an AI workflow useful and inspectable? Operational system: can he connect and run a workflow through failures and handoffs?
3. **Make proof easy to inspect.** Offer a case-study link, short narrated demo, and deeper technical evidence where it exists. Technical readers can inspect architecture and evaluation; nontechnical readers can understand the outcome first.
4. **Keep status prominent.** Client work, owned experiment, and concept product remain distinct. Label seeded data and simulated failures wherever those visuals appear.
5. **Use the existing nine-part case-study model.** Add a concise summary near the top and anchors to workflow, decisions, evidence, and learning. Do not invent a separate model per project.
6. **Connect each completed case to its documentation.** Link its article and video, then provide the approved fit-conversation CTA in context. The current case page ends with product exploration and supporting technology, which is an opportunity to make the next portfolio action clearer.
7. **Keep the index focused.** No empty coming-soon cards, artificial project count, filter UI for three stories, or restored discontinued projects just to fill space. The index's current “See selected work” footer action links to the same page; review whether that action is useful there.

These are proposals. No route, copy, schema, or visual changes were made in this analysis. Later implementation must verify desktop/mobile layouts, keyboard behavior, semantics, contrast, loading, and reduced-motion behavior as applicable.

## One project, several useful publications

Capture evidence during the work: initial assumptions, scope cuts, screenshots, short recordings, decisions, actual failures, test results, and observations. Build the final case from this record. Do not reconstruct a falsely smooth process after completion.

| Channel | Proposed role and artifact | Connection to the next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Portfolio | The durable case: situation, ownership, working product, key decisions, measured result, limits | Assess relevant work and schedule a fit conversation |
| Blog | One substantial explanation of the central product/engineering decision with reproducible evidence | Explore the case and inspect implementation details |
| YouTube | One approximately 5 to 8 minute narrated story: situation, working demo, difficult decision, failure example, result and limits | Try the sample or read the case |
| LinkedIn | Two or three standalone lessons written for founders/product leads, each grounded in one artifact | Reach the relevant case, not an unrelated homepage |
| X / Twitter | A concise technical thread or a few clips explaining one decision | Support discussion and direct readers to the full evidence |

Durations and quantities are planning suggestions. Start with one substantial source record and adapt it; there is no requirement to maintain four independent daily publishing programs. LinkedIn is the initial client-facing distribution hypothesis. Blog and video provide depth. X is a supporting channel unless actual audience response indicates otherwise.

Proposed Proofline titles, to use only once the described evidence exists:

- “The citation existed. It did not support the claim.”
- “What I changed between a working AI demo and a reviewable release.”
- “Why this AI workflow leaves the final decision with the reviewer.”
- “Where my claim-review tool still gets things wrong.”

Keep the prospective customer's problem central. Technical implementation earns attention when it explains a meaningful decision: an approval expiring after an edit, work surviving a failed run, or uncertainty remaining visible. A launch film can frame the story, but the product recording must show real behavior. Use captions and a readable written companion.

Use English first for the current international contracting audience unless channel evidence supports another choice. Spanish adaptations may support relevant local users without replacing that audience strategy.

Separate **product learning** from **client acquisition**. Record task completion, errors, repeat use, and improvement decisions for the product. Record relevant case visits, evidence engagement, qualified conversations, and the project people mention for the portfolio. Views and likes are distribution observations, not proof of client intent. Campaign links and a voluntary “which project brought you here?” question can support attribution; analytics changes remain future implementation.

## Suggested sequence and stop conditions

1. Improve the presentation and publication plan for the existing Armonía evidence. Assess whether the publishing system has enough actual operated evidence for a separate case before allocating another build.
2. Complete the XAP-54 selection discussion against current positioning. My recommendation is Proofline. Resolve the old candidate-list mismatch and Xavier's available effort and motivation before recording a final selection.
3. Write one narrow build brief under the existing delivery sequence. Define the user, source contract, useful workflow, acceptance evidence, evaluation baseline, distribution asset, and learning decision before choosing infrastructure.
4. Use the first week as a feasibility checkpoint, not a promise of a finished production product. If source support cannot be evaluated credibly, simplify the input contract or stop. Do not compensate for weak behavior by polishing the marketing page.
5. Complete one working release and its evidence package, then conduct the approved distribution and learning experiment. As a planning hypothesis, allow several focused weeks for product work, verification, and publishing; estimate properly after the first spike. Audience learning may take longer than implementation.
6. Choose a contrasting second build only after reviewing the result. If the publishing system already provides strong complementary evidence, another new product may be unnecessary.

A project earns a flagship slot when the central workflow works, its actual status is explicit, Xavier's ownership is clear, consequential decisions are inspectable, claims match the evidence, and there is a credible distribution and learning record. A concept with no adoption can still be published as a concept with an honest test result. A new revenue business is not a prerequisite for a credible work sample.
