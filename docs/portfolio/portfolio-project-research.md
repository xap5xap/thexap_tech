# XAP-103: fictional-client concept product research

Research date: September 7, 2026. Owner decision: accepted as complete on September 9, 2026; no product selected. Ledger: [XAP-103](https://linear.app/thexap/issue/XAP-103/research-identify-portfolio-projects-that-demonstrate-the-current), Portfolio Repositioning.

## Executive recommendation

**Recommend Tanda: a weekly preorder product for a fictional independent bakery.** A customer chooses from a small weekly bake, reserves available quantities, and receives a pickup reference. The baker sees the same orders as a preparation list and marks them ready and collected. The complete experience makes the commercial promise tangible: Xavier can shape a small business brief, create a credible brand and usable product, connect the customer journey to operations, and show what actually works.

The interesting work is deciding how a weekly batch should operate, then making that decision understandable and dependable across both sides of the product. The concept includes a working application, a visual identity, verified failure behavior, and a case that a prospective client can inspect. It should not expand into a general online store, delivery service, or restaurant platform.

**Shortlist Umbral as the strongest alternative:** a self-guided experience for a fictional small design exhibition. A visitor chooses a short or longer route, explores a handful of exhibits with audio and transcripts, and leaves with a saved collection. This offers more expressive interaction and editorial design, with a lighter operating burden. It provides less transactional engineering evidence than Tanda.

Relevo, a repair-service product, becomes competitive if Xavier has approximately 35 to 40 focused hours. It offers a strong service and ownership story but has more approval and status behavior to verify. Taller, a guided learning product, remains a reasonable alternative if teaching interests Xavier. Préstamo, an equipment-lending product, is parked for this one-week constraint because reservation and return behavior consume too much of the evidence and content budget.

These are recommendations about **portfolio usefulness**, not validated consulting demand. Existing products already cover much of every proposed workflow. For many real organizations, buying or configuring them would be the right recommendation. The fictional commission is a disclosed design assumption that permits a real work sample; it is not evidence that custom development is economically preferable.

### Search scope and stopping rationale

Discovery searched client-authored public briefs and official operator/product sources across repairs, shared equipment, cultural visits, learning, scheduled food orders and recording/archive experiences. Search terms combined those situations with booking, return, approval, visitor experience, project feedback, pickup, and official domains. Follow-up targeted the relevant existing software and the claim that one person must own all work. Some general tender searches yielded little; targeted visitor-experience searches produced S5. Generic vendor marketing, directories, Reddit anecdotes and third-party tender summaries were not used for material recommendations.

Research stopped after five distinct situations could be paired with a concrete workflow, an existing alternative, and a feasible or explicitly infeasible one-week boundary. Further general feature searches were repeating these patterns. Important gaps remain: no direct willingness-to-pay evidence for these fictional briefs, no buyer test of the work samples, no comparable measured build times, and no hands-on evaluation of the vendor alternatives. More desk research would not resolve those gaps reliably.

## Common scope for all candidate briefs

Each candidate below is a complete concept product within a small fictional commission, not a proposal to reproduce its cited source. The source supplies situation evidence; the fictional client supplies explicit assumptions; Xavier's later work must supply inspectable decisions and behavior.

The smallest coherent version includes an entry surface that explains the promise, the core interactive workflow, a persisted result, appropriate follow-through, empty/error states, responsive and keyboard operation, resettable sample records, a release rehearsal and handoff, and the documentation package. A separate marketing site is unnecessary if the product entry serves that role well.

Use one familiar implementation approach after selection. This research selects no framework, cloud, database vendor, AI provider or new paid tool. Estimates assume existing development tools, one language, one location or organization, a small seeded dataset and no third-party integration on the critical path. Role-based concepts still require real server-side checks even with seeded demonstration accounts. A cosmetic role switch is not permission evidence. Public anonymous demos would require additional isolation, abuse and privacy work; local access is the estimating baseline.

Incremental service spend can be zero for a self-contained local demonstration using already available tools. That excludes existing subscriptions, equipment and Xavier's time. Optional future public operation uses a **planning allowance**, not vendor pricing: approximately USD 0 to 30 per month for a small bounded demo, subject to provider choice, eligibility, limits and approval. If a selected design needs paid APIs, live messages or special infrastructure, re-estimate instead of silently consuming that allowance. Candidate-specific maintenance estimates below cover keeping an occasional demo working, not a real service business or service-level agreement.

Sample records, shop policies, prices, deadlines, curriculum and exhibit stories must visibly carry their fictional status. Do not manufacture a broken repository or fake business losses to stage a rescue. If showing a before-state, label it as the starting assumption. ## Fresh fictional-client product briefs

### Tanda: a weekly bake, from choosing to collecting

**Status: shortlisted; recommended for owner consideration.**

**Fictional client and brief.** Horno de Barrio is an invented independent bakery preparing one weekly collection batch. Its assumed starting situation is a menu shared as an image, customer requests in messages, and an owner reconciling quantities before baking. Its brief is to let customers reserve the current bake and give the baker one reliable preparation list. No lost sales, customer count or actual operational failure is asserted. The situation is informed by S9/S10; neither source establishes this bakery's constraints or a need for custom software.

**Consulting buyer and product users.** A founder or product lead shaping a direct-to-consumer or service-commerce product should recognize the connected decisions. The end users are a customer arranging a pickup and the baker preparing that batch. Local bakery owners are a possible audience, but not assumed to be the highest-budget consulting buyers.

**Promise and complete experience.** Reserve what will be baked and know when to collect it. The week-one version has three products, one collection window and one order deadline. The customer sees the bake and relevant item information, selects quantities, reviews the pickup promise, confirms a reservation, and receives a saved reference. The operator sees the same orders, an aggregated quantity list and a ready/collected action. The customer can revisit the order and cancel before the cutoff. Payment is explicitly on collection in the fictional operating model. There is no fake successful card payment.

**Product and craft decisions Xavier would own.** Decide between open-ended shopping and a weekly batch, when stock is committed, what cancellation means, what the customer must know before confirming, and whether a combined preparation/order view is clearer than a dashboard. Create a small identity, appetizing but truthful imagery, type hierarchy, product details, cart feedback and mobile confirmation. Test whether editorial warmth makes ordering clearer without obscuring availability. Product quantities and allergy-related text are fictional fixtures; a real baker or food specialist must supply and approve actual product and handling information. Xavier does not own food safety, kitchen capacity or fulfillment outcomes.

**Engineering and AI.** Persist orders and stock allocations together; calculate trusted totals on the server; enforce the deadline and operator permissions there. Use the final confirmation to allocate inventory, avoiding expiring cart holds. Capture readiness and collection in the same record. AI has no necessary runtime job. If used to create illustrative photography, label it and preserve asset provenance. No delivery routing, subscriptions, discount engine, payment provider, inventory purchasing or multi-location administration belongs in this version.

**Evidence, failure and handoff.** Demonstrate a real reservation reaching the preparation view and the later status reaching the customer. Test two sessions requesting the final item, duplicate confirmation, cutoff boundary, cancellation releasing stock exactly once, unauthorized operator actions and a failed save that must not display success. Inspect mobile, keyboard and quantity announcements. Rehearse startup from fixtures, export the batch, restore the seed state and explain recovery after an interrupted request. Keep a verified build identifier, test results and the actual demo recording. This proves the bounded local workflow, not real sales, food fulfillment or production scale.

**Effort and operation.** Estimate 26 to 34 focused hours, central plan 30, including documentation. Demo maintenance: roughly 0.5 to 1.5 hours per month when kept available. No external API or real customer access is needed. The main uncertainties are identity/asset iteration, reliable order mutation and any reusable account foundation. A familiar foundation is assumed; learning a new stack or payment platform invalidates this estimate.

**Learning, stop and simplify.** In a later authorized usability session, ask someone to reserve two items and explain the deadline, payment and pickup promise; ask a second person acting as baker to derive the preparation quantities. Record observed confusion rather than claim preference. Without participants, execute those tasks against fixtures and label the result as a self-review only. Cut multiple pickup slots, extensive photography and menu editing first. Stop expansion if the core order-to-preparation path is not working by the end of day 3. If standard pickup software satisfies the proposed real client's needs, recommend it; the concept remains a work sample, not a competitive replacement claim.

**Case and distribution.** Story: making the promise on the menu match what the kitchen must prepare. Show the customer/operator walkthrough and the last-item example, with fictional context visible. Product-user channel hypothesis: a bakery's existing local audience and pickup notice. Product CTA: reserve this week's bake. Buyer-facing asset: a short order-to-preparation film, one decision diagram and the verified demo. Buyer-facing CTA: bring the customer-to-operation transition you need to clarify; use the existing Schedule a meeting path only in separately approved publication. Useful learning is a qualified buyer describing a comparable need or asking to discuss scope, not likes on food imagery.

### Umbral: a small exhibition that visitors can explore their own way

**Status: shortlisted; strongest alternative for expressive product work.**

**Fictional client and brief.** Casa Umbral is an invented independent design space preparing a temporary exhibition of everyday objects. It has five fictional exhibits and draft interpretation text, but no defined digital visit. The brief is to create a short, usable visit that starts with the visitor's interest and ends with a collection they can revisit. This is an ambiguous new opportunity, not a rescue story. S5 supports the existence of digital visitor commissions; S6 shows a strong platform alternative.

**Consulting buyer and product users.** Buyers include founders and small product teams building culture, travel, learning or other content-rich experiences. The intended users are an exhibition visitor on a phone and an editor maintaining the five entries. This is not a claim that government procurement is Xavier's intended sales channel.

**Promise and complete experience.** Find a route through five objects and keep the ones that interest you. Choose a short or full route using deterministic editorial rules, open the first object, read or listen to its story, move to the next, save an object, and revisit the resulting collection. A tiny editor view changes an entry and previews a version before releasing it within the local demo. Persist visitor progress and the saved collection. The interface explains that navigation is within a fictional exhibit; it gives no real walking or accessibility-route advice.

**Product and craft decisions Xavier would own.** Define what a meaningful short visit contains, how much choice is useful, whether a linear or interest-led route works better, and how the visitor moves between physical object and screen. Create a distinctive editorial identity, object compositions, typography, motion with reduced-motion behavior, audio controls and readable transcripts. Limit production to five original/owned object visuals, five concise texts, and one short narrated sample reused appropriately. A real curator would own collection facts, interpretation, provenance and permissions. The demo uses invented exhibit descriptions, not fabricated museum authority.

**Engineering and AI.** Implement content/version validation, a preview/release distinction, saved progress and resumable collections. Audio starts only through deliberate user control and has a text fallback. Document behavior if media fails or local progress is cleared. Use deterministic route selection; runtime AI adds little to five edited stories. Exclude AR, GPS, generated curator voices, custom mapping, ticketing, donations, social accounts, a general CMS and promises of fully offline media. A connection interruption should degrade to available text or an honest retry state, not claim uncached content is available.

**Evidence, failure and handoff.** Show two different route choices and actual persistence after reload. Verify keyboard and screen-reader names, transcript access, reduced motion, missing media, an invalid exhibit ID, empty saved collections, and an editor change that cannot leak before release. Rehearse restoring the prior content version and exporting the entries. A visitor summary must distinguish opened or saved objects from an inferred successful museum visit. Deliver source content and an editor guide so the experience is maintainable without a custom creative agency.

**Effort and operation.** Estimate 24 to 32 focused hours, central plan 28. Demo maintenance: 0.5 to 1 hour per month, plus any content refresh. The highest risk is media production expanding past the allotted five entries. No real collection or institution access is required. One language and one audio sample are included; multilingual content, new commissioned art or cinema-quality production must be separately scoped.

**Learning, stop and simplify.** Later ask a participant to choose a route, find the transcript and return to a saved object; observe whether they understand the object story and where they are. A usability task is not evidence of cultural engagement or improved visitation. Without participants, record only functional and heuristic review. If the route is confusing, replace interest selection with short/full paths. Stop at three exhibits if necessary while preserving the entire visit/save/revisit/editor loop. If a real institution fits Bloomberg Connects, recommend assessing that before building custom infrastructure.

**Case and distribution.** Story: turning a loose exhibition idea into a usable visitor experience. The demonstration alternates visitor interaction with the editor's content decision. Product-user channel hypothesis: an exhibition entrance, event page or cultural newsletter. Product CTA: start a short visit. Reusable asset: a 30-second object-to-saved-collection clip plus a transcript/design comparison. Consulting CTA: discuss the content-rich product experience a team needs to shape. A buyer asking about product ownership is a stronger commercial signal than an audience praising the visuals.

### Definition of finished for the eventual concept

5. The case and all requested channel artifacts are prepared from actual work. There are no invented interviews, client approval, adoption, testimonials or commercial gains.
