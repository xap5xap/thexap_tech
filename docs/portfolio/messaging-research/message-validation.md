## 6. Five-second comprehension-test protocol

### 6.1 Test setup

Prepare two separate stimulus cards:

1. the homepage first screen from section 4.1;
2. the Upwork title and first two lines from section 4.2.

Use a neutral viewport appropriate to the channel. Do not show later sections or explain where the copy came from. Alternate stimulus order across reviewers to reduce order effects. If the same reviewer sees both stimuli, complete all recall and follow-up questions for the first stimulus before showing the second.

### 6.2 Moderator script

Read this exactly before each stimulus:

> I will show you a short piece of website or profile copy for five seconds. Please read it naturally. I will hide it, then ask what you understood. This is a test of the message, not of your memory or expertise. There are no right answers, and I will not correct or guide you.

### 6.3 Procedure

1. Record a neutral reviewer ID and reviewer segment. Do not record unnecessary personal data.
2. Record which stimulus is shown first.
3. Show the stimulus for five seconds.
4. Hide the stimulus completely.
5. Ask the five questions in section 6.4 in order.
6. Record each answer verbatim before asking the next question.
7. Do not paraphrase, complete the answer, offer choices, or reveal expected language.
8. After the five scored questions, ask the two unscored probes in section 6.5.
9. Record hesitation, visible confusion, and any word the reviewer explicitly says was unclear. Do not interpret it in the raw table.
10. Repeat with the second stimulus if applicable.

### 6.4 Scored questions

Ask exactly:

1. Who is Xavier for?
2. What problem does he solve?
3. What outcome does he help create?
4. Why should someone trust him?
5. What should the visitor do next?

### 6.5 Unscored diagnostic probes

Ask only after the five scored answers:

1. Which word or phrase, if any, was unclear or too abstract?
2. What did you think `one accountable senior owner` or `the agreed next state` meant?

These probes diagnose wording. Do not use them to repair an earlier answer.

### 6.6 Test integrity rules

- Keep exposure time at five seconds for the scored recall round.
- Do not count an answer learned from the second stimulus as a correct answer for the first.
- Preserve exact wording, including uncertain or incomplete answers.
- Record `no answer` when the reviewer cannot answer.
- Do not merge similar answers in the raw table.
- Do not present agent analysis as a reviewer response.
- Do not claim buyer validation from fewer than five target-relevant completed reviewers.
- A small purposive sample can reveal comprehension problems. It cannot estimate market prevalence, purchase intent, or conversion.

## 8. Comprehension scorecard

### 8.1 Scoring anchors

Score each answer from 0 to 2 using the stimulus-specific anchors below.

| Dimension | 0 points | 1 point | 2 points |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Audience | Wrong audience or no answer | Broadly says businesses, teams, founders, or product people | Founders, product leads, or small product teams with a working product or meaningful next release |
| Problem | Wrong problem, tool need only, or no answer | Mentions unclear work, release difficulty, or coordination | Says ownership is unclear or fragmented across connected product and engineering decisions for the next release |
| Outcome | Unsupported outcome or no answer | Says improve, finish, or ship a product | Says move the agreed next state toward a dependable release with clearer ownership and visible completion or verification |
| Trust | Invented proof or no visible reason | General senior or full-stack category only | Names visible bounded proof, such as at least nine years of full-stack delivery or another proof cue actually present in the stimulus |
| Action | Wrong action or no answer | General contact or learn more | Homepage: schedule a meeting or see selected work. Upwork: send a message or tell Xavier where the product stands |

### 8.2 Misclassification codes

Record any of these separately from the numeric score:

- `AI-FIRST`: reviewer thinks Xavier's primary category is AI specialist, prompt engineer, agent engineer, or AI consultant;
- `GENERIC-DEV`: reviewer sees only generic development capacity with no ownership distinction;
- `AGENCY`: reviewer assumes an agency or undisclosed delivery team;
- `GUARANTEE`: reviewer hears a guarantee of release, reliability, speed, savings, or business outcome;
- `EVERYTHING`: reviewer thinks Xavier personally performs every specialist task or owns every future product result;
- `EMPLOYMENT-FIRST`: reviewer thinks the main offer is job seeking rather than founder-led product work;
- `NO-ACTION`: reviewer cannot identify what to do next.

### 8.3 Decision thresholds

Use these thresholds only after at least five target-relevant completed reviewers exist.

For each stimulus:

- at least 80% score 2 on audience;
- at least 80% score at least 1 on problem, and at least 60% score 2;
- at least 80% score at least 1 on outcome, and at least 60% score 2;
- at least 70% score at least 1 on trust, using only proof visible in the stimulus;
- at least 80% score 2 on action;
- the mean total is at least 8 of 10;
- no more than 20% receive any one material misclassification code;
- no reviewer interprets the copy as a guarantee, runtime-AI claim, provider attack, or universal solo-delivery claim.

Decision rule:

- **Proceed unchanged:** every threshold passes and no credibility boundary is breached.
- **Revise and retest:** any essential dimension misses its threshold or a repeated unclear phrase appears.
- **Stop for strategy review:** reviewers consistently identify a different audience, category, problem, or commercial promise.
