# Portfolio Research, Strategy, and Concept Backlog

## Purpose

This document preserves the audit and brainstorming that led to the portfolio direction encoded in the repository's `AGENTS.md`. `AGENTS.md` contains the durable operating rules; this file contains the analysis, candidate ideas, and proposed implementation sequence that can evolve as the site changes.

The approved positioning hierarchy and route-copy direction now live in `docs/portfolio/positioning-and-messaging.md`. That decision record controls when this earlier research language differs from the approved message.

## Strategic conclusion

The portfolio should not become a collection of hypothetical landing pages. It should become a deliberately curated set of **real products, owned experiments, and clearly labeled concept products** that prove Xavier can take an opportunity from idea to audience.

The desired belief is:

> Xavier can discover the opportunity, shape the product, create the brand and experience, build the system, produce the launch story, distribute it, and learn from the market.

A concept earns a flagship case study only when it demonstrates a coherent product system: problem, audience, product judgment, experience, implementation, story, distribution, evidence, and learning.

## Audit of the current portfolio

### 1. The portfolio contradicts the current positioning

The homepage promises “From idea to AI product,” architecture-first AI, production readiness, and end-to-end delivery.[2] The About page reinforces the AI Solutions Architect position and describes ownership across agentic systems and the application stack.[3]

The projects index does not substantiate that promise. It presents the legacy project collection under a generic “Projects” heading without an editorial narrative, featured hierarchy, project status, outcomes, AI contribution, or distribution evidence.[1]

### 2. The index communicates names rather than value

The current card system gives each legacy project essentially equal visual importance. A visitor cannot tell from the index:

- what problem the product solves;
- who the audience is;
- whether the work is live, owned, or conceptual;
- what Xavier personally owned;
- what changed because of the work;
- whether AI was involved;
- how the product reached an audience;
- why a specific case study deserves attention.

The redesign should replace the undifferentiated logo grid with a small number of narrative flagship cards and move credible older work into a secondary engineering archive.

### 3. The detail pages are résumé entries, not case studies

Representative detail pages follow the same sequence: product, role, responsibilities, technologies, and URL. Selfstudy labels the role “Fullstack developer” while the stated responsibility is Cypress end-to-end testing.[4] Haiku Deck lists implementation and prototyping tasks but provides no user problem, decisions, constraints, outcome, or distribution story.[5]

This structure emphasizes tools touched rather than product judgment. Technology should remain supporting evidence, not the narrative spine.

### 4. Armonía is the clearest immediate opportunity

The existing thexap case study describes Armonía primarily as a therapist invoicing application and Xavier as a fullstack developer.[6] The live Armonía experience now communicates an emotional promise, a three-step path to care, Carla's credibility, areas of practice, conversion actions, and client reassurance.[7]

The gap between the old case study and the current product makes Armonía the best first flagship. It can truthfully demonstrate product strategy, service positioning, UX, application engineering, brand evolution, cinematic media, conversion, content, and distribution.

### 5. Individual case studies are weak shareable assets

The current legacy project pages use generic page framing and do not offer project-specific narrative hooks for proposals, LinkedIn sharing, direct outreach, or sales conversations. Each flagship should have a distinct title, description, social preview, hero visual, and outcome-led summary.

## Recommended positioning

### Primary direction

> **From idea to audience. I design, build, launch, and distribute AI products.**

Supporting copy:

> Product strategy, brand, UX, full-stack AI engineering, cinematic storytelling, and distribution, handled as one coherent product system.

“From idea to AI product” remains conceptually present in the supporting AI-product promise, but should not remain as a competing slogan. “Audience” closes the loop and communicates that shipping software is not the end of Xavier's responsibility.

## Recommended projects-page information architecture

### 1. Positioning hero

Use a heading such as **Selected products and systems** with a brief explanation that the page contains real products, owned experiments, and clearly labeled concepts.

Visible project labels should include:

- Live product
- Client work
- Owned experiment
- Concept product
- AI system
- Brand and distribution

### 2. Three flagship stories

Use large editorial cards rather than equal logo tiles. Each card should contain:

- a product image, cinematic frame, or workflow, not merely a logo;
- an honest work-type and status badge;
- the audience and intended transformation;
- a one-sentence outcome or hypothesis;
- ownership tags such as `Strategy`, `Brand`, `AI`, `Engineering`, `Film`, and `Distribution`;
- one verified proof point when available;
- a clear case-study action.

### 3. Capability-through-evidence strip

Show the complete cycle:

**Discover → Position → Design → Build → Launch → Distribute → Learn**

Each stage should connect to a real artifact in one or more case studies. The site should prove “end-to-end” rather than merely claim it.

### 4. Secondary engineering archive

Retain only older projects that still provide credible evidence of production experience. Present them compactly so they support Xavier's nine-year track record without defining his current identity.

### 5. Contextual conversion section

Recommended direction:

> **Bring me the product problem, not a finished specification.**

> I can help shape the opportunity, build the system, create the launch story, and get it in front of the right audience.

## Flagship case-study anatomy

Every flagship should answer:

1. **What changed?** The outcome or intended transformation.
2. **For whom?** The audience and their problem.
3. **Why this product?** The insight, bet, or hypothesis.
4. **What did Xavier own?** Strategy, design, engineering, AI, media, and distribution.
5. **How does it work?** Product workflows and system behavior.
6. **What decisions mattered?** Constraints, trade-offs, rejected directions, and risks.
7. **How did it reach people?** Launch and distribution artifacts.
8. **What evidence exists?** Real outcomes, or an honest experiment and measurement plan.
9. **What was learned?** Reflection and next iteration.

## Recommended initial portfolio slate

### 1. Armonía: real flagship

**Proof gap filled:** whole-product ownership and emotionally resonant storytelling.

Potential evidence set:

- business and audience problem;
- product and service positioning;
- patient-facing journey;
- operational application behind the service;
- brand evolution;
- Google Flow cinematic sequence;
- appointment and content funnels;
- real constraints, decisions, and measured evidence where available.

### 2. Publishing engine: owned internal system

**Proof gap filled:** invisible automation, agentic workflows, approval gates, and distribution infrastructure.

Potential evidence set:

- idea or issue intake;
- research and drafting workflow;
- generated visual assets;
- human review and approval;
- Contentful publication;
- social distribution;
- status tracking and failure handling;
- examples of assets produced by the system.

### 3. Echoes: concept product

**Concept:** An AI oral-history studio that interviews a family member and turns the conversation into a written story, audio keepsake, and short illustrated film.

**Demonstrates:**

- voice and multimodal AI;
- emotionally sensitive UX;
- cinematic storytelling;
- consent, privacy, and trust decisions;
- a coherent consumer product.

**Distribution motion:** A personalized mini-documentary designed to be shared with relatives, creating a natural family-referral loop.

### 4. Pilot Room: concept product

**Concept:** An AI launch operator for independent creators that turns a product brief into positioning, campaign assets, channel experiments, and a learning dashboard.

**Demonstrates:**

- multi-agent orchestration;
- tool integrations;
- human approval gates;
- evaluation and observability;
- distribution strategy embedded in the product.

**Distribution motion:** A public “launch a product in seven days” challenge, teardown videos, reusable templates, and a waitlist experiment.

### 5. Quito Alive: concept evolution

**Concept:** Reimagine Quito360 as a multilingual AI city companion that creates thematic routes using local stories, maps, imagery, and cultural context.

**Demonstrates:**

- evolution of an older project rather than erasing its history;
- geospatial interfaces and multimodal AI;
- local cultural storytelling;
- a mobile product experience;
- partnership-oriented distribution.

**Distribution motion:** Cinematic neighborhood shorts, creator itineraries, QR-based discovery, and a tourism-partner kit.

## Additional concept backlog

### Mentor Canvas

An AI-assisted mentoring workspace that converts goals, session notes, and observed blockers into adaptive plans. It should emphasize mentor judgment and human accountability rather than autonomous advice.

### Proofline

A claim-checking and citation assistant for creators. It could demonstrate retrieval, source provenance, human review, uncertainty handling, and quality evaluation through a browser workflow or publishing integration.

### Field Notes

An AI research companion that turns observations, interview fragments, and recurring frustrations into structured opportunity areas without prematurely generating solutions.

### Demo Reel

A system that turns a working product into demos, launch clips, documentation, screenshots, and channel-specific social assets while preserving one coherent message.

### Freelance Radar

A lead-qualification and proposal workspace for independent experts. It could connect opportunity discovery, fit scoring, proposal generation, portfolio evidence, and follow-up without presenting automation as a substitute for judgment.

### Sazón Signals

A culturally grounded food and nutrition concept centered on realistic Ecuadorian meals, household constraints, and progressive learning. It should avoid generic “AI meal planner” positioning and require credible subject-matter boundaries.

## Idea selection scorecard

Score shortlisted ideas from 1–5 on:

- positioning proof;
- product depth;
- AI/system depth;
- visual storytelling potential;
- distribution potential;
- distinctiveness from the rest of the slate;
- feasibility;
- credibility of evidence.

A beautiful landing page is not sufficient reason to select an idea. Prefer concepts that can produce a working vertical slice, a defensible system explanation, a strong launch artifact, and an honest learning signal.

## Recommended build order

1. Redesign the projects information architecture and work-type taxonomy.
2. Publish the new Armonía flagship case study.
3. Document the publishing engine as an owned product system.
4. Score the concept backlog and select one concept.
5. Build its product vertical slice, launch story, and distribution experiment together.
6. Measure the result and document the learning.
7. Only then select a contrasting second concept.
8. Move weaker historical projects into a compact archive.

Do not build five concepts in parallel. One complete concept with a functioning experience, system explanation, launch film, campaign, and test is stronger than five polished homepages.

## Truth and evidence rules

- Never present concept work as a real client engagement.
- Never invent users, revenue, testimonials, conversion lifts, or production constraints.
- Mark simulated data and interfaces explicitly.
- Separate observed facts, assumptions, hypotheses, and measured outcomes.
- Explain what Xavier personally owned.
- Use AI only where it performs a meaningful product job.
- Document failure modes, safeguards, and evaluation where AI behavior matters.
- Treat distribution as product work rather than a post-launch afterthought.

## Remaining open decisions

- Which three projects should be visually featured at launch.
- Whether the portfolio remains backed by `data/projects.json` or moves to a richer content model.
- Which concept should be the first full build after Armonía and the publishing engine.
- What verified outcomes and assets can be published for each real client project.
